What is this?

Nachtunruhe is a small computer (a Raspberry Pi) with a microphone at the window, installed 4 meters above coordinate 47.3883170, 8.5214150. It listens to the street 24 hours a day and keeps a diary — but a very special one: it never stores any sound or recording. Instead, every second it only writes down two things: how loud it was, and what kind of sound it recognised (a car, a voice, a bird, music…). Think of it as a librarian who listens to the street and only takes notes, never recordings.

Source code

Nachtunruhe is open source. Find the code at github.com/hostcenter/noise-analysis.

Why does it exist?

When a loud motorcycle wakes you up at 2 a.m., the natural questions are: How often does this happen? Every night or just today? Is it worse on weekends? Since when? Guessing is frustrating — this diary answers with facts. After a few weeks the views show clear patterns: which hours are the noisy ones, which days are quiet, and which sound types dominate your street.

Chart axes and navigation

Each view is tailored to inspect noise patterns across different time scales and dimensions:

  • Daily (Disturbance & Sleep Profile):
    • Disturbance & Sleep Continuity Cards: Evaluates night rest quality (Restful, Moderate, Disturbed, Severely Disturbed) based on awakenings count and quiet intervals in the core sleep window (00:00–07:00), displays longest continuous quiet period, and identifies the loudest night peak.
    • Primary Annoyance Drivers: Ranks the top offensive sound sources using a psychoacoustic annoyance formula (night penalty multiplier × loudness contrast × duration).
    • Horizontal axis (X): Time of day running from 00:00 to 24:00 in 1-hour increments. Night quiet periods (00:00–07:00 and 22:00–24:00) are shaded with a soft violet background band.
    • Left vertical axis (Y): Cumulative hourly noise level (acoustic load = episodes × loudness above noise floor), stacked into three color bands: Audible (<62 dB SPL, green), Loud (62–67 dB SPL, yellow), and Very loud (≥67 dB SPL, red).
    • Right vertical axis (Y): Worst Peak sound level (Lmax in calibrated dB SPL) for each hour, ensuring sudden shocks and loud drive-bys are prominently visible even if total episode count was low.
    • Core Sleep Awakenings Log: Lists the loudest intrusive events during the night window (00:00–07:00) with exact timestamp, recognized sound category, peak dB, and duration.
    • Interaction: Use the left/right arrow buttons or the Left (←) and Right (→) keyboard keys to step through individual days. Filter by noise family (All, Speech, Vehicles) via the dropdown.
  • Noisemap (2D Heatmap):
    • Horizontal axis (X, top): Hours of the day from 00 to 23 across the top. Day hours (07:00–21:59) follow a green brightness gradient; night hours (22:00–06:59) follow a violet brightness gradient; the current in-progress hour cell is shown in greyscale.
    • Vertical axis (Y): Calendar dates (e.g. Mon 05.10.), arranged chronologically with newest days at the top.
    • Cell color: Represents the raw hourly acoustic load (sum of episode loudness above the ambient noise floor) — brighter cells indicate higher noise load. Hovering over any cell displays the exact date, time window, and noise level.
  • Noisescape (3D Surface):
    • X axis (width): Hour of day (00:00 to 23:00, with tick marks every 3 hours).
    • Y axis (depth / airstrip): Calendar days starting from October 4th, extending into the background.
    • Z axis (vertical height): Hourly noise level (acoustic load) rendered as a continuous bicubic Catmull-Rom spline surface.
    • Color scale: Chromatic gradient mapped to noise level: green (low/quiet) → yellow (medium) → red and dark red (high noise level).
    • Interaction: Press the Up (↑) and Down (↓) arrow keys to glide forward toward recent days or backward toward October 4th like an airstrip runway. Click and drag to orbit / tilt; scroll to zoom.
  • Last 5 min (Live strip):
    • Horizontal axis (X, top): Peak loudness in calibrated dB SPL on a logarithmic display scale (log(dB + 56)), with tick marks at 45, 50, 55, 60, 65, and 70 dB SPL. Tinted background columns mark the green (≤62 dB), yellow (62–67 dB), and red (≥67 dB) ranges.
    • Vertical axis (Y): Continuous scrolling time over the past 5 minutes (300 seconds), with the oldest reading at the bottom and incoming real-time measurements arriving at the top.
    • Trace & badges: The solid black curve tracks peak decibel levels over time. Identified sound categories appear as black badges ("pills") pinned at their exact time and loudness.

How loud is "loud"? (the dB numbers)

The microphone is calibrated against a reference SPL meter, so the numbers are true dB SPL at the window. The color code everywhere: green = audible (up to 62 dB), yellow = loud (62–67 dB), red = very loud (67 dB and above). For orientation: a quiet night lull sits around 45–50, a passing car 60–70, a loud motorcycle 80+.

What counts as one episode?

One real-world occurrence = one episode. When a car passes by, the computer briefly recognises several sound categories at once — but they are merged into one line: start time, the clearest type (at its loudest moment), the peak level of the whole episode, and how long it lasted. Then the notebook stays shut for 10 seconds. So one passing car ≈ one line in the diary, not five. Sounds that are impossible here (cows, owls, whales, artillery…) and pure noise-floor labels are filtered out automatically.

Noise families

The computer originally distinguishes over 500 sound types — far too many to read. Where a view needs grouping, Nachtunruhe sorts them into a few families: Motor (cars, motorcycles, trucks, engines), Human (voices, shouting — and animal sounds like birds, dogs and cats), and Other (sirens, trains, aircraft, music, doors). Most views pool all families into one picture.

Logfile format

The system stores all measurement data in logs/ using the JSON Lines (.jsonl) format — plain text where each line is a self-contained, valid JSON object. This makes logs streamable, easy to parse with standard Unix tools (grep, jq), and resilient against crashes. In accordance with strict privacy principles, raw audio is never written to disk.

Two complementary logfiles are recorded:

1. events.jsonl (Acoustic episodes)

Emitted at the end of each acoustic episode (after 2 seconds of quiet, capped at 120 seconds maximum). Episodes are subject to a 10-second refractory window to prevent double-counting continuous noises.

{"ts":"2026-11-04T20:45:10.920Z","event":"Speech","confidence":0.969,"spl_db":-42.2,"spl_db_max":-38.9,"duration_s":19.5}
FieldTypeDescription
tsstringISO 8601 UTC timestamp of episode onset (millisecond precision).
eventstringIdentified sound category name from the AudioSet ontology (e.g. Speech, Car, Motorcycle, Siren).
confidencenumberYAMNet classifier probability (0.000 to 1.000) at the episode's clearest/loudest frame.
spl_dbnumberA-weighted decibels at the moment of peak classifier confidence.
spl_db_maxnumberPeak A-weighted decibel level observed across the entire episode.
duration_snumberDuration of the episode in seconds, from onset until the quiet threshold.

2. spl.jsonl (1-second sound pressure levels)

Emitted continuously every second to track the ambient sound floor and broad frequency spectrum.

{"ts":"2026-11-04T20:47:08.649Z","spl_db":-50.9,"spl_db_min":-51.1,"spl_db_max":-50.7,"bands":{"low":-64.4,"mid":-54.0,"high":-54.7}}
FieldTypeDescription
tsstringISO 8601 UTC timestamp of the 1-second aggregation interval.
spl_dbnumberMean A-weighted sound pressure level over the 1-second interval.
spl_db_minnumberMinimum instantaneous sound level recorded within the second.
spl_db_maxnumberMaximum instantaneous peak sound level recorded within the second.
bands.lownumberLow-frequency band energy in dB (63 Hz – 250 Hz; engine rumble, bass).
bands.midnumberMid-frequency band energy in dB (250 Hz – 1,000 Hz; human speech, tire roll).
bands.highnumberHigh-frequency band energy in dB (1,000 Hz – 8,000 Hz; brake squeal, tire hiss, birds).

Privacy

No audio is ever recorded, stored, or transmitted. No internet connection is needed — everything runs inside the apartment on the Pi. What exists are anonymous numbers and labels: "30.09., 07:15, Vehicle, 71 dB". Nobody can reconstruct a conversation or a voice from this data.

Day-to-day

Everything runs automatically: the diary keeps writing even when this page is closed, it survives power cuts, and a copy of the log is pushed off-site (Cloudflare R2) every hour. Measured errors (e.g. the microphone was touched) can be removed again with a small purge tool. Just open this page whenever you want to look.
