About Nachtunruhe

What is this?

Nachtunruhe is a small computer (a Raspberry Pi) with a microphone at the window. It listens to the street 24 hours a day and keeps a diary — but a very special one: it never stores any sound or recording. Instead, every second it only writes down two things: how loud it was, and what kind of sound it recognised (a car, a voice, a bird, music…). Think of it as a librarian who listens to the street and only takes notes, never recordings.

Why does it exist?

When a loud motorcycle wakes you up at 2 a.m., the natural questions are: How often does this happen? Every night or just today? Is it worse on weekends? Since when? Guessing is frustrating — this diary answers with facts. After a few weeks the views show clear patterns: which hours are the noisy ones, which days are quiet, and which sound types dominate your street.

Sidebar filters

22-06 hours switches the episode views between all hours (00–24h) and night hours only (22:00–06:59) — the small badges next to the view names show which views react to it (they dim when all hours are shown). "Louder than" sets where "loud" begins for the 3D colors and the Weekday grids.

How loud is "loud"? (the dB numbers)

The loudness numbers (dB) here are relative — the microphone is not calibrated against an official meter. Use them to compare: −60 is very quiet (nighttime lull), −50 is normal street murmur, −40 is clearly loud (a truck or shouting), −30 and above is very loud at the window. Bigger number (closer to zero) = louder. The color code everywhere: green/blue = audible, yellow/orange = loud, red = very loud.

What counts as one episode?

One real-world occurrence = one episode. When a car passes by, the computer briefly recognises several sound categories at once — but only the loudest moment is written down, together with how long the episode lasted. Then the notebook stays shut for 10 seconds. So one passing car ≈ one line in the diary, not five. Episodes with a duration ≥ 10 seconds are the ones that really drag on. Sounds that are impossible here (cows, owls, whales, artillery…) and pure silence are filtered out automatically.

Sound families

The computer originally distinguishes over 500 sound types — far too many to read. Nachtunruhe sorts them into a handful of families: Human (voices, conversations — including animal sounds like birds, dogs and cats), Motor (cars, motorcycles, trucks, engines), Music, and Other. All views use these same families.

Privacy

No audio is ever recorded, stored, or transmitted. No internet connection is needed — everything runs inside the apartment on the Pi. What exists are anonymous numbers and labels: "18.09., 23:12, Motor, −38 dB". Nobody can reconstruct a conversation or a voice from this data.

Day-to-day

Everything runs automatically: the diary keeps writing even when this page is closed, it survives power cuts, and the data stays on the device. Just open this page whenever you want to look.
