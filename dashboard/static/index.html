About Nachtunruhe

What is this?

Nachtunruhe is a small computer (a Raspberry Pi) with a microphone at the window. It listens to the street 24 hours a day and keeps a diary — but a very special one: it never stores any sound or recording. Instead, every second it only writes down two things: how loud it was, and what kind of sound it recognised (a car, a voice, a bird, music…). Think of it as a librarian who listens to the street and only takes notes, never recordings.

Why does it exist?

When a loud motorcycle wakes you up at 2 a.m., the natural questions are: How often does this happen? Every night or just today? Is it worse on weekends? Since when? Guessing is frustrating — this diary answers with facts. After a few weeks the views show clear patterns: which hours are the noisy ones, which days are quiet, and which sound types dominate your street.

How loud is "loud"? (the dB numbers)

The microphone is calibrated against a reference SPL meter, so the numbers are true dB SPL at the window. The color code everywhere: green = audible (up to 62 dB), yellow = loud (62–67 dB), red = very loud (67 dB and above). For orientation: a quiet night lull sits around 45–50, a passing car 60–70, a loud motorcycle 80+.

What counts as one episode?

One real-world occurrence = one episode. When a car passes by, the computer briefly recognises several sound categories at once — but they are merged into one line: start time, the clearest type (at its loudest moment), the peak level of the whole episode, and how long it lasted. Then the notebook stays shut for 10 seconds. So one passing car ≈ one line in the diary, not five. Sounds that are impossible here (cows, owls, whales, artillery…) and pure noise-floor labels are filtered out automatically.

Noise families

The computer originally distinguishes over 500 sound types — far too many to read. Where a view needs grouping, Nachtunruhe sorts them into a few families: Motor (cars, motorcycles, trucks, engines), Human (voices, shouting — and animal sounds like birds, dogs and cats), and Other (sirens, trains, aircraft, music, doors). Most views pool all families into one picture.

Privacy

No audio is ever recorded, stored, or transmitted. No internet connection is needed — everything runs inside the apartment on the Pi. What exists are anonymous numbers and labels: "30.09., 07:15, Vehicle, 71 dB". Nobody can reconstruct a conversation or a voice from this data.

Day-to-day

Everything runs automatically: the diary keeps writing even when this page is closed, it survives power cuts, and a copy of the log is pushed off-site (Cloudflare R2) every hour. Measured errors (e.g. the microphone was touched) can be removed again with a small purge tool. Just open this page whenever you want to look.
